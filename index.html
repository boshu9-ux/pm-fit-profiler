Product Manager Fit Profiler

A structured self-assessment for working out which kind of product management fits you — which archetype of the role, which organisational environment, and which problem domain. 60 questions, about 10–12 minutes.

What it measures

Person–environment fit theory holds that satisfaction and performance come less from ability alone than from the match between a person's dispositions and needs and what a given environment supplies and demands. This instrument samples four layers of that match:

LayerConstructs
Disposition
20 items		Five trait dimensions drawn from the Five-Factor model, narrowed to the facets that predict PM performance: ambiguity tolerance, execution discipline, assertive influence, consensus orientation, emotional steadiness.
Work values
16 items		What the job has to supply for you to stay motivated: autonomy, mission, technical craft, commercial drive. Closest in spirit to career-anchor and work-values research.
Context preference
12 items		Structural fit: tolerance for low-process environments, pace and risk appetite, tolerance for stakeholder density.
Domain interest
12 items		Vocational interest in six problem families, which maps to industry shortlists.
Read this before you start. This is an original instrument built on public-domain frameworks. It has not been validated against a normative sample, so scores are ipsative — they compare your scales against each other, not against other people. It is a well-structured mirror for reflection and a good input to a career conversation. It is not a selection tool, a clinical measure, or a substitute for a professionally validated assessment, and it should not be used to make a hiring decision about anyone.
How to answer. Respond as you actually are on an ordinary week, not as you'd like to be or as you are on your best day. Go fast — first instinct is usually the more accurate one. Some items are worded in reverse on purpose, so read each one properly. Nothing is sent anywhere; everything stays in this page.

Built on public-domain constructs (Five-Factor model via IPIP-style item writing, vocational interest theory, work-values research, the Competing Values Framework). Contains no items from any proprietary or trademarked instrument. Ipsative scoring; no normative sample.
